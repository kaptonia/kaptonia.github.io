Verification is done by the receiver. “Verify” in the sidebar has three tabs, “Verify grant”, “Verify record” and “Look up a ledger”, for Recorder and User alike. Verifying only reads: it reads the input and the chain, and sends not a single transaction.

Verify grant

Put in all three things, then press “Verify” once:

  1. Grant: paste a grant code, or press “Choose a file…” to pick a grant file.
  2. File (optional): the work you received.
  3. Terms (optional): the terms file.

Under “Advanced options” you can point each hop of the grant chain at the issuer’s ledger folder; “Nodes (one per line, optional)”, “Registry contract (optional)” and “From block” come filled in with the main network from Settings and are open to change, setting the main network alone; “Check as of (seconds, optional)” left empty means chain time.

Which networks a check reads

Verify grant, Verify record, Look up a ledger and due diligence read the main network plus every one of the read-only networks in Settings › Network, and say for each chain whether it was read. Grants and records may be anchored on any chain you have added, and one check reads them all.

  • A network that could not be read is named, with “Unreachable” or “Fingerprint mismatch” (the code at its registry is not the pinned build).
  • When a chain could not be read, an item with no anchor on the chains that were read shows “Chain unreadable right now” or “Chain not read”, and the next step is to check again later; its anchor may be on the chain not read, so it is left unjudged rather than called “Not on chain”.
  • Verify grant with read-only networks set up and no main network still checks.

Where the issuer’s ledger comes from

The full check needs the issuer’s ledger. The app looks for it in this order:

  1. This machine: the issuer’s ledger on this same machine.
  2. The vault: ledgers carried by grant files you added, and upstream folders you saved.
  3. A record kit: the kit or grant file you point to, and the ledger carried by the grant file being checked.
  4. A publish address: the issuer’s https address; each file is fetched, and the kit must pass its check before it is used.

The result card has one “Ledger source: …” line per hop, naming where it was found, or “None” if nowhere; you can also press “Replace…” or “Add…” to give an https address, record kit, ledger folder or grant file yourself. A ledger from a publish address shows “From https://… · N files · verified”, with “Fetch again”.

The six checks

Grant signature
This entry was signed by the issuer’s key, and it is a grant.
Issuer ledger intact
The issuer’s ledger links up entry by entry, with its chain whole.
In the issuer’s ledger
This grant is an entry in that ledger, and the key that signed it may write to the ledger.
Confirmed on chain
This grant, or some entry after it, is on chain.
Within validity
The moment of the check falls between the start and end the grant states.
Not revoked
Having read the issuer’s whole ledger, the grant still stands.

A check still awaiting its answer shows a grey light with the reason beside it: “Issuer ledger missing”, “Issuer ledger unreadable”, “No nodes configured”, “Chain unreadable right now”, “Not yet confirmed on chain” or “Chain time unavailable”; below, each kind of gap gets one sentence saying what to do next.

Each hop of a sublicense gets its own six lights, and between hops a light “Hop N links to hop N+1”: the downstream ledger must have been opened by the grantee the upstream grant names. The chain passes when every hop passes.

The result

The result card starts with three lines:

LineShows
Grant“All six checks passed”, “Has gaps” or “Failed”, tagged “Active”, “Undecided” or “Invalid”
File“Match” or “Mismatch” against the work hash the grant points to; for several hops, the last hop
Terms“Match” or “Mismatch” against the terms fingerprint in the grant

Anything not given reads “Not provided”, and anything that could not be read reads “Unreadable”. The summary at the bottom is one of three: “All match” (the grant passed all six checks, and the file and terms, where given, match), “N do not match”, or “Not everything could be checked” (whatever was checked matches, but the grant has gaps or a file could not be read).

“Has gaps” is a tier of its own, meaning no answer yet, and it always counts apart from passing. When you meet it, check again in a few minutes; if you need the grant urgently, ask the other side for the missing material.

To judge a check “Failed” takes evidence in hand, such as a revocation or a wrong signature; to judge “Not revoked” takes having read the complete record. When the record has gaps, that check shows a grey light and the grant is judged “Has gaps”.

Checking a received file

When you receive the work itself, put it into the “File” box on the Verify grant page and press “Verify” together with the grant: the app computes the SHA-256 of the file’s bytes and compares it with the work hash the grant points to. “Details” shows the byte count, the computed digest and the expected digest side by side. After changing the file, press “Verify” again. The User home tile “Check a received record” shows the last result.

The record verifier

“Verify record” under “Verify”. Drop in a record kit folder, a grant file or a single record file, and it is checked as soon as it is in; an https:// publish address can be given under “Advanced options”. The result card has three lights:

Record kit
Whether the kit is intact and its signatures valid; a single file or a folder of entry files is verified as a single file.
Anchor proofs on chain
How many match the chain and how many are not on chain yet; “Not compared with the chain” when the chain could not be read, no network is set up, or a network was not read.
Issuer ledger
The ledger check result; “This is part of the ledger” when the kit carries only part of it.

The “Record by record” table below has one row per record in the kit: whether the original in the kit matches its recomputed fingerprint, the on-chain state, and the first anchor block time. With a record hash filled in under “Advanced options”, three depth readings for that record appear as well. The result card names the networks read this time; chain ID, registry contract, and each record’s first-anchor block number and full transaction are under “Details”.

The conclusion is one of two: a green “All match” when nothing mismatches, or “N mismatches.” otherwise, a cue to confirm with the sender before accepting. A chain not read counts as a mismatch: a failed chain read counts once, and each record on a network not read counts once. So when the kit’s network has not been added, no network is set up or no node answers, the conclusion shows mismatches however intact the kit is.

When the kit’s network has not been added. When a kit says which chain it is anchored on, and that chain with its registry contract is neither the main network nor among the read-only networks, the kit is still checked and the anchor-proof light reads “Not added”; the page shows the chain’s name, “Stated by the author” and “Add this network in Settings”, with “Add”. “Add” opens Settings › Network with a new row whose chain ID, registry contract and start block are filled in; you fill in the nodes.

The verification result file. Each kit, grant file or publish address verified writes a result file in the machine folder under kits/verified/, named 0x<sha256 of the kit’s manifest file>.json; verifying again replaces it whole, and a kit that holds is left unwritten when no chain was read this time. It holds whether the kit holds, each record’s earliest counted anchor, the networks read and not read this time, and the digest of the core that judged; the format is in Files and formats. The file is plain, for other apps to read; ZIKARON writes it and never reads it back, and it changes no verdict.

Looking up a ledger

Fill in “Author address” and press “Read”: the app scans the chain for that address’s anchor proofs and looks for its ledger in the four places above. The main network must be set up first; any read-only networks are read as well. “Overview” has four tiles for the ledger status, the record count, grant history and the last time on chain; “Entry list” lists the other ledger entry by entry, and a click on a row opens a read-only detail. When anchor proofs turn up and the ledger itself is missing, the overview says so, and the record count reads “Not obtained”.

Due diligence

A User does due diligence on “Look up a ledger”: fill in the record hash and the validity needed under “Advanced options”, and “Overview” gains a “Grant conflict check”, which tells you whether the period you need overlaps active grants the author has already issued; its “Details” give the “Key lineage” and “Max depth”. “Save snapshot” writes this due diligence to a JSON snapshot for your own records; a snapshot is not proof.

The scope of a result

Every check rests on one concrete reading: which chains, which registry contract, from which block, and which addresses. “All passed” means that within what was read, all six checks passed. To widen the scope, add read-only networks, or fill in “Advanced options” and check again.

Verifying writes three things on this machine: changes to the address book, snapshot files written by “Save snapshot”, and the verification result files written by “Verify record”.
