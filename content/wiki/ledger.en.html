Your ledger is a record book of your own: entries are written one after another, and every entry stays exactly as written. Recording adds a file to the ledger; putting it on chain lets anyone check its time.

Recording

  1. Drop a file onto Home or the Records page to open the “New record” card. Folders and Git repositories work too.
  2. Fill in “Record name”; left empty, the file name is used.
  3. Press “Add to ledger”, then press it again on the confirmation card.

What the app records is the file’s content fingerprint: the same file always gives the same fingerprint, and changing one byte gives a wholly different one. The file itself stays where it is, and the app records its fingerprint. To record several files at once, drop them together into the drop area inside the “New record” card; each gets a record of its own.

  • Keep the original files safe. To prove something later, you show the very file that matches the fingerprint.
  • Record names and notes travel with your ledger to whoever you give it to, and stay for good.
  • For short content that is easy to guess (a date, an amount), combine it with some random text in one file before recording it.

Putting it on chain

Putting entries on chain sends your new records in one transaction to the blockchain. From then on, anyone can check that they existed by that moment.

  1. New records wait under “Pending”. Click the “Pending” card on Home, which opens the “Put on chain” card; or press “Put all on chain” in the “Pending” segment of the Records page.
  2. The card shows the number of entries and the gas cap.
  3. Press “Confirm and send”. A few minutes later the state turns “Confirmed”.

All the records in a batch travel in one transaction for the price of one, so sending several together costs less. You can close the app meanwhile; it picks up the wait when you open it again.

  • When the balance falls short, the message at the top says how much is needed and how much is there; press “Copy address” on it and send a little ETH.
  • When a batch stays out of a block for long, “Resend with higher fees” appears at the top of the Ledger page; it sends the batch again with higher fees, and the batch is paid for once.
  • To send after every write, turn on “Auto put on chain” in Settings › Network.

The more often you put records on chain, the more precise their time: once a day makes it precise to the day.

States

StateMeaning
PendingQueued, waiting for you to send it
Waiting to go on chainSent, waiting to get into a block
ConfirmingIn a block, waiting for the app to check it
ConfirmedChecked against the chain: done
Reverted, Not sentThis attempt did not go through; the record stays queued and can be sent again

The ledger status at the top of the Ledger page reads “OK” or “Problems”. Every few minutes the app compares your ledger with the chain; when it says “Problems”, click it to see which item.

When something is wrong

A ledger only grows at the end. For an entry already written, there are two ways:

  • Add an explanation: “Add note…” in the “More” menu of the Ledger page puts a note on that entry.
  • Delete the record: press “Delete record…” at the bottom of the record’s detail page. The record is struck through and leaves the list of records you can grant; the original entry and grants already issued stay as they are.

Networks

Each identity uses one network, and its records go to that chain:

NetworkUse
Ethereum mainnetThe default, the most widely read
Arbitrum One, OP MainnetFees usually a small fraction of mainnet’s
Sepolia testnetFree to try, with test coins

Choose it in the “Network” field when you make an identity. The app comes with public nodes; to use nodes of your own, press “Edit nodes…” in Settings › Network. To verify other people’s records anchored on another chain, add that chain under “Read-only networks” in Settings › Network.

VPN and proxy. “Proxy” in Settings › Network decides how the app’s connections to nodes go out, one setting for every identity on this computer:

System
The default. Follows the system’s proxy settings; when the system uses an automatic configuration script (PAC), the app connects straight.
Off
Always connects straight.
Custom proxy
Enter http://host:port or socks5://host:port and press “Save”. The proxy has to work without a user name and password.

Nodes on this computer are always reached straight. The line below, “New connections now: …”, says whether connections go straight or through which proxy; after a change, the next connection follows the new setting.

  • A VPN in global mode that carries all traffic: keep “System”.
  • A VPN that only sets a system proxy: use “System”.
  • Nodes still out of reach: find the VPN’s HTTP or SOCKS5 proxy address in the VPN app and enter it under “Custom proxy”.

When the command line works through the desktop, it uses these settings too.
